02 / BIOINFORMATICS Computational Practice

Reference Genome Alignment and Sequence Analysis Using NGS Tools

Comparative whole-genome sequencing analysis involving Saccharomyces cerevisiae YJM451, focusing on reference-based sequence alignment, variant processing and comparative analysis of variant call sets.

Workflow
  • Quality assessment of sequencing reads using FastQC.
  • Read trimming and adapter removal using fastp.
  • Reference genome indexing and read alignment using Bowtie2.
  • SAM/BAM processing and alignment handling using SAMtools.
  • Variant calling and SNP/indel processing using BCFtools.
  • Variant annotation and raw-versus-trimmed variant comparison using Python.
Technical tools
FastQC fastp Bowtie2 SAMtools BCFtools Python FASTA SAM/BAM Linux/WSL
Selected findings

Performed SNP/indel annotation and comparison of raw and trimmed variant call sets. The comparative analysis reported a Jaccard similarity of 99.50% across the evaluated call sets.

Computational project involving reference genome alignment and downstream NGS data analysis.

03 / INHALATION TOXICOLOGY Research Project

Inhalation Toxicology Assessment of Lithium-Ion Battery Thermal Runaway Emissions via MPPD Modeling

Investigated the potential respiratory deposition of particles generated during lithium-ion battery thermal runaway using Multiple-Path Particle Dosimetry (MPPD) modelling.

Research Internship · CSIR-Indian Institute of Toxicology Research (CSIR-IITR), Lucknow · 8 June – 8 September 2025

Methodology
  • Particle collection and size characterization using MOUDI cascade impactor data.
  • Calculation of mass median aerodynamic diameter (MMAD) and geometric standard deviation (GSD).
  • Respiratory deposition modelling using MPPD v3.04.
  • Comparison of regional deposition across adult and elderly, male and female lung models.
  • Particle characterization using SEM/SEM-EDX and ion chromatography analysis of smoke extracts.
Technical tools
MPPD v3.04 MOUDI MMAD GSD SEM-EDX Ion Chromatography ConsExpo Aerosol Science
Research outcome

The measured aerosol parameters included an MMAD of 0.66 µm and a GSD of 1.8. These parameters were used as inputs for MPPD simulations to investigate regional respiratory deposition across different age- and sex-specific lung models.

Research conducted during an internship at CSIR-IITR, Lucknow, under the supervision of Dr. Jyotsna Singh.

04 / MICROBIOLOGY & QUALITY CONTROL Research Project

Evaluation of Microbial Load of Herbal Raw Materials and Finished Product

Research Internship · Central Ayurveda Research Institute (CARI), Jhansi · 15 January – 15 February 2025

Evaluated the microbial load of herbal raw materials and finished products to assess microbiological quality and potential contamination using conventional microbial enumeration techniques.

Methodology
  • Preparation of Soybean Casein Digest Agar (SCA) and Sabouraud Dextrose Agar (SDA).
  • Sample preparation and serial dilution from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁵.
  • Microbial enumeration using pour plate and spread plate techniques.
  • Assessment of Total Viable Count (TVC), Total Aerobic Microbial Count (TAMC) and Total Yeast and Mould Count (TYMC).
  • Selective-media streaking and observation of suspected microbial contaminants.
  • Microbiological quality assessment of herbal raw materials and finished products.
Technical tools and methods
Microbial Limit Test TVC TAMC TYMC SCA SDA Serial Dilution Pour Plate Spread Plate Selective Media
Research outcome

Performed microbial enumeration and documented colony characteristics from herbal samples. Selective-media streaking was used to examine suspected contaminants, including colonies suspected to be Staphylococcus aureus. The work contributed to microbiological quality assessment and laboratory QC reporting.

Training conducted under the supervision of Dr. Hemant Soni, Central Ayurveda Research Institute, Jhansi.

05 / MOLECULAR DOCKING In Silico Research

Mechanistic Pathway of Polystyrene Micro/Nanoplastics on Human Biological Targets: An In Silico Molecular Docking Study

Investigated potential molecular interactions between styrene tetramer, used as a representative polystyrene oligomer, and selected human proteins associated with biological and toxicological pathways.

Methodology
  • Selection of human protein targets associated with biological and toxicological pathways.
  • Preparation of protein structures and ligand parameters.
  • Use of styrene tetramer as a representative polystyrene oligomer.
  • Molecular docking using SwissDock and Attractive Cavities scoring.
  • Ligand parameterization using SwissParam and interpretation of predicted docking interactions.
Selected biological targets

The investigation included proteins associated with inflammatory responses, oxidative stress, epithelial barrier function and cellular signalling. Selected targets included IL-6, TGF-β, TLR4, CD44, HSP70 and VEGF-A, among other proteins.

Technical tools
SwissDock SwissParam Attractive Cavities PyMOL Chimera Molecular Docking Protein-Ligand Analysis
Selected findings

The predicted docking free-energy estimates included −5.91 kcal/mol for IL-6 and −5.90 kcal/mol for TGF-β. These values represent computational predictions and should not be interpreted as experimental binding affinities.

Computational research investigating potential interactions between polystyrene oligomers and human biological targets.
